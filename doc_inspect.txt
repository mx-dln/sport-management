P23: INTRODUCTION
P24: Project Context
P25: Web-based information systems have become widely adopted in organizations to support the management of projects, records, and institutional information. A systematic review by Rebuglio, Zenezini, Ottaviani, and De Marco (2025) identified Project Management Information Systems (PMIS) as the dominant software used for organizing project information, monitoring project implementation, managing documentation, and supporting project-related decision-making. The review, which analyzed 108 studies published over the previous five years, also emphasized the growing application of PMIS across different organizations despite variations in terminology. Likewise, Urmeneta, Clemente, Polinio, Guino, Sablawon, and Alberca (2025) developed a Web-Based Extension Services Management Information System specifically for State Universities and Colleges in the Philippines. The researchers used centralization of submission of proposals, documentation of projects and documentation of extensions for their study on the digital management of extension programs. The above-mentioned studies show that the use of web-based project and extension management information systems has become common in managing extension records in institutions of higher learning.
P26: Similar to this, the Extension and Training Services (ETS) Office of Isabela State University-Cauayan is the office of the university that handles the planning, coordination, implementation, monitoring, documentation, and evaluation of extension programs and community engagement initiatives. This office works together with the faculty members, program leaders, project leaders, study leaders, extension coordinators, partner agencies and community stakeholders to manage the extension activities as per the university policies and regulatory requirements. As a part of its routine work, the office maintains the records of extension programs, projects, project components, assigned extensionists, proposals, accomplishment reports, certificates, and other relevant documents for institutional reporting and evaluation.
P27: However, the management of the extension programs and projects in the Extension and Training Services Office is carried out mainly through manual documentation and separate physical and electronic files. The storage of records in different folders and documents makes the process of retrieving, updating, monitoring and consolidating the information cumbersome and error prone. Monitoring of the status of extension programs, tracking of project information, organizing of supporting documents, preparation of accomplishment reports, and generating institutional reports becomes a challenging task, especially when several extension projects are implemented at once in different colleges.
P28: General Objective
P29: The general objective of this study is to develop a Web-Based Project Management Hub for Optimized Extension Training Services at ISU-Cauayan to facilitate the management and monitoring of extension programs, projects, project components, extensionists, proposals, and institutional reports.

P48: Scope and Delimitations
P49: This study focuses on the development of a Web-Based Project Management Hub for Optimized Extension Training Services at ISU-Cauayan. Specifically, the developed system provides a web-based tool which helps the Extension and Training Services (ETS) Office in managing and monitoring the extension programs/projects, project components, extensionists, proposals, accomplishment reports, certificates, and supporting documents.
P50: In particular, the system involves the evaluation of the current processes and challenges of the ETS Office in handling activities and records related to extension programs/projects. The system consists of a web-based tool that provides centralized management of relevant information/documents associated with extension programs/projects. Also included in the system is the role-based routing that assists in the proper routing of proposals/project-related information depending on the authority of the users.
P51: In addition, the system includes automated notification capabilities intended to provide updates to extensionists and program leaders about proposal approvals and project statuses. This feature facilitates the communication and monitoring process throughout the implementation of extension programs/projects, as well as the project components. Accomplishment reports, certificates, and supporting documents necessary for ETS office can also be organized and managed through the system.
P52: Target users of the system include ETS personnel, program leaders, project leaders, and faculty extensionists of Isabela State University-Cauayan Campus. The developed system will be assessed based on the selected ISO/IEC 25010 software quality criteria such as Functional Suitability, Usability, Performance Efficiency, and Security. Feedback from the users will be gathered in order to determine the acceptance of the system.
P53: Delimitation of the Study
P54: The study is confined to the management and monitoring of extension programs/projects, project components, proposals, accomplishment reports, certificates, supporting documents, and related activities within the Extension and Training Services Office of ISU-Cauayan. It does not involve other university operations and management functions other than the activities related to extension and training services.
P55: The developed system is intended only for authorized users, which include ETS personnel, program leaders, project leaders, and faculty extensionists. Determining the system functions and information accessible depends on the assigned roles and permissions of the users. Users/organizations outside ISU-Cauayan are excluded from the study unless there is a need for their participation within the recorded extension activities.
P56: Automated notification capability is limited only to the system-generated notifications regarding proposal approvals and project statuses. It does not serve as an alternative to the existing official university communication channels and administrative processes requiring outside the developed system.
P57: Moreover, evaluation of the system is confined only to the Functional Suitability, Usability, Performance Efficiency, and Security under ISO/IEC 25010, as specified in the objectives of the study. Other software quality characteristics not included in the stated evaluation criteria are outside the scope of the study.

P63: This chapter presents a review of related literature and studies, both foreign and local, relevant to the development of the A Web-Based Project Management Hub for Optimized Extension Training Services at ISU-Cauayan. P64: Foreign Studies
P65: Project Management Information Systems in Organizations
P66: Based on the study of Fernandes, Sousa, Tereso, and O'Sullivan (2021), Project Management Offices (PMOs) play an important role in coordinating activities within university research centers. The researchers examined the roles and functions of PMOs through a literature review and a survey involving 370 participants engaged in collaborative research and development projects. The study identified different levels of PMO maturity and emphasized functions associated with proposal development, project execution, governance, reporting, knowledge management, and coordination. Moreover, the researchers found that university research organizations were receptive to establishing structures that could improve project management and project performance. This is relevant to the present study because extension training activities at ISU-Cauayan also involve proposal preparation, implementation, monitoring, reporting, and coordination among different stakeholders. Hence, the proposed Project Management Hub can serve a similar coordinating function by bringing essential project activities and information into a centralized platform. 
P67: According to Suharno and Imraan (2024), a website-based Project Management Information System was developed using HTML, PHP, and MySQL to support project management activities. The system enabled project progress to be updated weekly through a dashboard and provided integrated online project document archiving. Furthermore, project histories, problems, solutions, discussions, and photographs were recorded in the system so that the information could be used as a reference for future project implementation. This study demonstrates the value of combining project monitoring and document management within one web-based environment. Thus, the findings strongly support the present study's plan to provide ISU-Cauayan extension personnel with a centralized platform for project progress, documentation, activity history, and supporting files. 
P68: From the research of Nuraisya, Hapsari, and Safri (2025), the design of a Project Management Information System was based on the information requirements of a web-based project. The study emphasized that timely, accurate, and centralized project information is important for project monitoring and management. Moreover, the researchers used web-based dashboards to present project progress in a form that stakeholders could easily understand. The study highlights the importance of identifying stakeholder information requirements before developing a project management system. Therefore, this supports the present study's approach of determining the actual information and workflow requirements of extension coordinators, project leaders, trainers, and administrators at ISU-Cauayan before implementing the proposed Project Management Hub. 
P69: Furthermore, Lizar and Rosmita (2024) applied the Critical Path Method (CPM) and Program Evaluation and Review Technique (PERT) to a project management information system for a university website project. The system was designed to support project planning and scheduling by identifying critical activities and estimating project duration. The findings indicated that the application helped manage project activities according to the required deadline. This is relevant to the proposed system because extension training projects also contain activities that must be completed within specific schedules. Hence, project scheduling and activity tracking can help extension personnel identify upcoming activities, delayed tasks, and important project deadlines. 
P71: Web-Based Project Monitoring and Progress Tracking
P72: Based on Musva and Ramadhani (2025), a web-based project monitoring system was developed because conventional project supervision through WhatsApp and email resulted in fragmented communication, limited progress traceability, and unstructured documentation. The proposed system provided real-time, structured, and integrated monitoring of project activities. The study demonstrates that centralized project monitoring can improve the availability and organization of project information. Moreover, the findings indicate that web-based monitoring can reduce the difficulties associated with relying on separate communication channels. This is directly relevant to the present study because extension training projects involve multiple activities, personnel, and documents that can become difficult to track when information is distributed across different communication platforms. Thus, the proposed Project Management Hub can provide a centralized location for monitoring extension project progress. 
P73: In addition, Tiwi (2025) developed a web-based project monitoring information system for CV Prima Jaya Solution in Indonesia. The study identified problems associated with conventional project monitoring, including delayed reporting, inaccurate information, difficulty in field monitoring, and coordination problems between teams and management. The developed system enabled real-time monitoring of project progress and was evaluated using black-box testing, with all 30 test scenarios successfully executed. This supports the present study because extension training services likewise require accurate progress information and effective coordination between project personnel and administrators. Therefore, real-time project status and systematic activity monitoring can help ISU-Cauayan personnel identify project conditions and respond to delays more efficiently. 
P74: According to Pane and Silalahi (2025), project progress is an important indicator of project success and must be continuously monitored in relation to time, scope, cost, and quality. Their study developed a web-based project monitoring information system after identifying problems associated with manual monitoring using Microsoft Excel, including delayed updates, inconsistent information, and difficulty in consolidating project information. The researchers applied the Web Development Life Cycle in developing the system. This study is relevant to the proposed Project Management Hub because extension projects similarly generate information that needs to be updated, consolidated, and monitored throughout implementation. Hence, the proposed system can reduce dependence on scattered spreadsheets and provide a centralized view of extension training project status. 
P75: Furthermore, Fergina et al. (2025) emphasized that project monitoring is necessary to ensure that project implementation remains aligned with established schedules, objectives, and resources. Although the technological context differs from the present study, the research reinforces the importance of accessible, real-time project information for decision-making. Therefore, the proposed Project Management Hub can similarly provide administrators with updated project information through dashboards and monitoring interfaces without requiring them to manually consolidate project records.

P103: The reviewed literature and studies consistently demonstrate the growing importance of web-based information systems in managing complex projects and institutional extension services. Based on Fernandes et al. (2021), project management structures within universities require functions that support proposal development, project execution, coordination, reporting, governance, and knowledge management. Similarly, Suharno and Imraan (2024) demonstrated that web-based project management systems can integrate progress monitoring, project documentation, activity histories, and online document archiving. Thus, these studies establish the importance of centralizing project information within a single system. 
P104: Furthermore, studies by Musva and Ramadhani (2025), Tiwi (2025), and Pane and Silalahi (2025) demonstrate that manual or fragmented project monitoring can result in delayed updates, inconsistent information, limited traceability, and coordination difficulties. Web-based monitoring systems can address these problems by providing centralized project information and real-time or regularly updated progress information. Hence, the proposed Project Management Hub can help ISU-Cauayan extension personnel monitor the status of training projects without relying on separate spreadsheets, messages, or manually consolidated reports. 
P105: Moreover, the literature demonstrates that extension services have distinct management requirements. The study of Urmeneta et al. (2025) is particularly significant because it was conducted in a Philippine State University and College and focused specifically on a Web-Based Extension Services Management Information System. The system centralized proposal submission, approval, document sharing, data collection, communication, and reporting. Similarly, Estrera et al. (2022) developed a web-based document management system for a university extension office. These studies confirm that Philippine university extension offices can benefit from digital systems that centralize extension-related processes and records. 
P106: In addition, Arago (2023) demonstrates the importance of providing a centralized gateway for extension-service stakeholders, while De Jesus and Buenas (2023) demonstrate how dashboards, KPIs, interactive visualizations, event management, reports, and target monitoring can support extension-service decision-making. Therefore, the proposed Project Management Hub can combine project-management functions with extension-specific monitoring and analytics. 
P107: The literature also establishes that monitoring and evaluation are essential components of extension-service management. The University of Northern Philippines Extension Manual identifies project monitoring information such as implementing unit, venue, beneficiaries, collaborators, benefits, evaluation results, beneficiary comments, and issues raised by stakeholders. Furthermore, CHED Memorandum Order No. 18, Series of 2025 provides revised guidelines and instruments for client satisfaction surveys of extension projects in State Universities and Colleges. Thus, the proposed system should not end with project scheduling and activity tracking; it should also support monitoring, evaluation, feedback, and reporting. 
P108: Furthermore, the reviewed literature indicates that centralized project documentation is important for organizational continuity. Project proposals, reports, photographs, activity histories, evaluation records, and supporting documents can provide evidence of project implementation and can serve as references for future projects. Hence, a document-management component within the proposed Project Management Hub can improve accessibility, organization, and preservation of extension training records. 
P109: However, although several existing studies address web-based project management, extension-service management, document management, monitoring, and analytics, these functions are frequently addressed as separate systems or within different institutional contexts. The 2025 Web-Based Extension Services Management Information System is closely related to the present study, but its primary focus is on extension proposal processing, approval, document sharing, and communication. Meanwhile, the MET Online Services study focuses strongly on extension-service monitoring, KPIs, dashboards, and analytics. Project management studies, on the other hand, emphasize project progress, scheduling, documentation, and coordination. 
P110: Therefore, a gap can be identified in the integration of project management and extension training management into one web-based platform specifically designed for the operational requirements of ISU-Cauayan. The proposed system aims to bridge this gap by combining project proposal and approval, project planning, task assignment, training scheduling, participant management, document management, project-progress monitoring, notifications, monitoring and evaluation, dashboards, and report generation.
P111: Thus, the proposed A Web-Based Project Management Hub for Optimized Extension Training Services at ISU-Cauayan is grounded in current research demonstrating that centralized web-based systems can improve project coordination, documentation, monitoring, communication, data accessibility, and decision-making. The proposed system builds upon these findings but adapts them to the specific context of extension training services at ISU-Cauayan. In doing so, the system is intended not merely to digitize existing records but to provide an integrated environment for planning, implementing, monitoring, evaluating, and documenting extension training projects.
P112: Concept of the Study
P114: Figure 1. Input-Process-Output (IPO) Diagram of the Web-Based Project Management
P115: Hub presents the conceptual framework of the proposed Web-Based Project Management Hub for Optimized Extension Training Services at ISU-Cauayan. The framework consists of three major components: Input, Process, and Output, which are interconnected to illustrate how the information, requirements, and resources of the Extension and Training Services Office are transformed into a centralized web-based project management system. The framework is aligned with the study's objective of managing extension programs, projects, project components, extensionists, proposals, documents, accomplishment reports, certificates, notifications, and user accounts.
P116: The Input component consists of the information and requirements needed for the development and operation of the proposed system. These include user information, extension program information, project information, stakeholder information, proposals, documents and files, monitoring and evaluation data, and system settings and parameters. The study identifies authorized users such as the System Administrator, ETS Personnel, Program Leaders, Project Leaders, and Faculty Members participating in extension activities. The inputs also include information related to extension programs and projects, project components, assigned extensionists, proposals, accomplishment reports, certificates, and other supporting documents. These inputs provide the information necessary for organizing extension-related records and determining the requirements of the proposed Project Management Hub.
P117: The Process component represents the activities performed by the proposed system in transforming the collected information into organized and useful project-management information. The first process is User Authentication and Authorization, where the system verifies user credentials and provides access according to assigned roles and permissions. This supports the study's requirement for role-based access control for administrators, ETS personnel, project leaders, and faculty members. The second process is Data Input and Validation, where information is entered into the system and checked for accuracy and completeness. The third process is Data Management, which involves storing, organizing, updating, and maintaining program, project, stakeholder, and document records. These processes address the existing condition in which extension records are maintained through manual documentation and separate physical and electronic files.
P118: The succeeding processes involve Monitoring and Tracking, Document Management, Report Generation, and Notifications and Alerts. Monitoring and Tracking allow authorized users to follow the status and progress of extension programs and projects. Document Management provides an organized means of storing and accessing proposals, accomplishment reports, certificates, photographs, and other supporting documents. Report Generation compiles available information into reports and analytical information that can support monitoring, evaluation, and institutional reporting. Notifications and Alerts provide reminders or alerts regarding project deadlines, updates, and important activities. These processes are consistent with the study's identified need to improve project monitoring, documentation, reporting, and coordination through a centralized platform.
P119: The Output component represents the information, records, and services generated by the proposed Web-Based Project Management Hub after the inputs have been processed. These include organized program and project lists, project details and status information, accomplishment reports, a centralized document repository, dashboards and analytics, notifications and reminders, and exportable reports. The system is intended to provide the Extension and Training Services Office with centralized information for monitoring project implementation, managing records, generating institutional reports, and supporting decision-making. Program leaders and project leaders can also use the system to monitor project progress, manage project information, submit proposals, update accomplishments, and upload supporting documents.
P120: Overall, the IPO framework shows the relationship between the information and requirements collected from the Extension and Training Services Office, the systematic processes used to manage, monitor, document, and analyze the information, and the outputs produced by the proposed Web-Based Project Management Hub. The framework reflects how the existing extension management requirements and challenges can be translated into centralized system functions that support program and project management, documentation, monitoring, reporting, and coordination. It therefore provides a conceptual guide for developing a web-based platform that is aligned with the requirements and documentation practices of the Extension and Training Services Office of Isabela State University-Cauayan.

P128: Requirement Analysis
P129: The requirement analysis was based on the existing operations of the Extension and Training Services (ETS) Office of Isabela State University-Cauayan and on the requirements identified in the study. The ETS Office handles the planning, coordination, implementation, monitoring, documentation, and evaluation of extension programs and community engagement activities. It maintains records of programs, projects, project components, assigned extensionists, proposals, accomplishment reports, certificates, and other supporting documents. The present study identified manual documentation and separate physical and electronic files as major sources of difficulty in retrieving, updating, monitoring, and consolidating extension information.
P130: The requirements analysis therefore focuses on centralizing extension-related records and workflows. The proposed system requirements include user authentication and role-based access, program and project management, component and extensionist management, proposal management, document management, accomplishment reports, certificate generation, reports and analytics, notifications, audit logs, and user management. The authorized users considered in the study are the System Administrator, ETS Personnel, Program Leaders, Project Leaders, and Faculty Members participating in extension activities.

Data Flow Diagram
P132: Figure 3 Existing Procedure Data Flow Diagram 
P133: The existing procedure begins when Program Leaders, Project Leaders, and Faculty Extensionists prepare program information, project proposals, supporting documents, accomplishment reports, and certificate requests. These records are submitted to the Extension Training Services Office through manual or separate electronic channels. ETS Personnel review the submissions, request revisions when necessary, file the records, and prepare status updates and institutional reports. Project information is stored in physical folders and separate electronic files, which makes retrieval, status monitoring, and report consolidation difficult.
P134: The proposed system will centralize these data flows. Authorized users will submit information through the web application. ETS Personnel will review and route proposals, update project status, manage documents, and generate reports and certificates. The system will send status notifications through the configured notification services and store records in a centralized database and document repository.
P135: Figure 4 Fishbone Diagram
P136: The Fishbone Diagram identifies the major contributing causes of inefficient management and monitoring of extension programs and projects in the ETS Office. The causes are grouped into People, Process, Documents, Technology, Monitoring, and Reporting. This classification shows that the problem is not limited to a single activity but is associated with how users, procedures, records, technology, monitoring activities, and reporting tasks interact.

The identified causes include unclear role routing and dependence on manual follow-ups; manual submission, review, and delayed approvals; scattered and duplicate records; absence of a centralized system and limited automated notifications; difficulty tracking project status and components; and time-consuming report consolidation with possible errors. These findings support the need for a centralized web-based project management hub that provides role-based routing, document management, project monitoring, notifications, and reporting functions.

P181: Design of Software/Systems
P182: The system design translates the requirements into a centralized web-based architecture. The design emphasizes role-based access, modular management of extension records, centralized documentation, monitoring, and reporting. The interface should provide a dashboard appropriate to the user's role, with navigation to only the modules authorized for that user. Forms should use validation rules to reduce incomplete or inconsistent data. Project records should maintain status information and timestamps so that progress can be monitored and reported.
P183: The design also incorporates document and activity traceability. Project proposals, accomplishment reports, supporting documents, monitoring results, certificates, and related files should be associated with the relevant program or project. Audit logs should record significant user actions, while notifications should provide system alerts for relevant deadlines, status changes, or required actions. The dashboard should summarize information such as active projects, upcoming training activities, completed activities, pending proposals, project progress, participants trained, and evaluation results.
P184: Software Development Methodology
P185: The proposed system will use an Agile and iterative software development approach. This approach is appropriate for the project because requirements may be refined through consultations and user feedback as the system modules are developed. The development will proceed through repeated cycles of planning, design, development, testing, review, and refinement. The researchers will first identify requirements, prioritize system modules, design the database and interfaces, implement selected functions, test them, gather feedback, and refine the system before proceeding to the next iteration.
P186: Development and Testing
P187: Development will be conducted by converting the approved requirements and system designs into working modules. The researchers will establish the database structure, implement authentication and role-based permissions, develop the major management modules, integrate document and reporting functions, and connect the modules through the central database. The interface will be tested at different screen sizes to ensure that authorized users can access the system through standard web browsers.
P188: Testing will be performed throughout development rather than only after all modules have been completed. Errors identified during testing will be documented, corrected, and retested. User feedback will be considered during refinement so that the final prototype addresses the operational requirements of the ETS Office.
P189: Data Gathering Procedure
P190: The researchers will gather requirements through consultation, observation, and document analysis. Initial consultations will identify the roles of personnel, current workflows, records maintained, and problems encountered in managing extension projects. Observation will focus on how proposals, project information, supporting documents, accomplishment reports, and institutional reports are prepared, updated, stored, and retrieved. Document analysis will examine available forms, reports, templates, certificates, and other records that can be represented in the proposed system.
P191: The information gathered will be organized into functional and non-functional requirements. The researchers will validate the requirements with authorized stakeholders before using them as the basis for system design and development. During prototype evaluation, the researchers will provide the developed system to the identified evaluators and collect their assessment using an evaluation instrument based on applicable ISO/IEC 25010 quality characteristics.
